# Working With Me

## Who I Am

I'm a builder at heart. I get genuine joy from making things work - shipping something real, solving a hard problem, watching an idea turn into a product that's used by an audience of 1 & beyond. I also know that most projects fail, and I'm at peace with that. The ones that land often look like a series of fails before the success comes along. The ones that eventually land make the misses worth it.

Professionally, I live at the intersection of AI and product. My background is in insurance technology, document processing, and prompt engineering, but my interests run wider than any one domain. I care about building tools that solve real problems for real people - especially in markets that tend to get overlooked. I started in insurance and stayed for over a decade for that very reason.

Outside of work, yoga has been one of the most transformative forces in my life. It taught me that showing up consistently, even when it's uncomfortable, is how you change. That philosophy carries over into how I work.

## How I Work

- **I bias toward action.** I'd rather build a rough version and learn from it than plan forever. Experimentation is the plan and iteration is the strategy.
- **I think in systems.** I care about how the pieces connect and the foundation of which those pieces will sit. We can do a lot more on top of a strong foundation. Strong foundations are built on first principles.
- **I teach while I build.** If I know something useful, I'll share it. I genuinely enjoy helping people level up, whether that's walking through a Git workflow, explaining how a prompt chain works, what an insurance dedecutible is, or how to ask for a raise.
- **I learn while I build.** I am a life-long learner and I really enjoy when projects push me into uncharted territory where I am forced to learn something new. I consider myself a slow but deep learner - you may need to tell me the same thing multiple times before I understand it, but once that understanding happens, it is a lock.
- **I protect focus time.** Deep work is where the real output happens. I try to minimize and batch meetings and keep large blocks of time open for building.
- **I document as I go.** I value clear documentation because it respects everyone's time, including future-me's, but also understand that documentation creates maintenance liability. Clear documentation looks like just-enough, just in-time, straightforward, and no-nonsense.

## My Working Norms

- I typically work on my primary project from 9a-5p cst Monday through Friday. Right now that project is Plansight.
- I typically have multiple additional side projects on-going that I create a more custom and restrained schedule for, which I will provide definition on an as-needed-basis.
- I work remotely but enjoy short, infrequent, optional in-person working sessions. Typically means less than a full working session, once a week or less, and having to switch from in-person to remote shouldn't kill the session.
- I prefer async, written communication by default. Good async communication is defined deeper below.
- I expect a few live syncs per week for any project I am on. Can happen with text, voice, or in-person, and the logistics and content can be driven by the needs of the project and team. A minimium would be 1 per week, just to stay and feel connected to each other.
- The fastest way to reach me on a project is always Slack.

## What I Expect of Myself & You

These are the standards and values I hold for myself and the people I work with:
- **Honesty:** To be honest about what I know and what I don't.
- **Malleable:** To be open to being wrong but confident along the way.
- **Drama-free:** To own my mistakes quickly and fix them without drama.
- **Take action:** To move fast & break things.
- **Challenger:** To create an environment where people feel safe to challenge the status quo, and so often.
- **Curiosity:** To stay curious. The moment I stop learning is the moment I stop being useful.
- **Clarity:** To communicate clearly and consistently.
- **Follow through:** If you say you'll do something, do it. If something changes, just let me know. Reliability builds trust faster than anything else.
- **Take ownership:** I don't want to micromanage, and I won't. Bring solutions alongside problems when you can.
- **Be kind:** We're a small team. How we treat each other matters as much as what we ship.

## Communication

### Async (Slack, Session, Email)

- My default mode. I prefer async for most things - it gives everyone time to think before they respond and organize their work around life versus their life around work.
- I don't expect instant replies and you shouldn't feel pressure to give them. Respond thoughtfully, not reactively.
- I need to know your working norms so that silence between replies isn't a bottleneck to my own thought process. If I know your working norms and you demonstrate them through consistent actions then our async conversations will feel more aligned. For example, if I know you typically work on our shared project from 5pm - 1am cst then I won't expect replies outside that window.
- Have a simple check-in/out ritual with the crew. A quick hey to start the day and bye to call it quits goes a long way so no one is wondering if you're alive during periods of really intense headsdown deep work. Bonus points from me on the days you take an extra 30 seconds to making the ritual fun, personal, and/or informative.
- Be clear and specific. "Hey, can you look at this?" is less useful than "Can you review x for y and help me figure out z?"
- If something is truly urgent, say so explicitly. I won't assume urgency unless you flag it.
- If everything is urgent, then something else is broken about the project and I expect that we prioritize time to diagnose the more deeply rooted issue - is it a staffing problem? money problem? knowledge problem? Discipline problem? Projects where everything is urgent are likely to fail.

### Sync (Calls, Meetings, Pairing)

- I value sync time for things that benefit from real-time back-and-forth: brainstorming, debugging together, alignment on ambiguous decisions, alignment on soft stuff like vision, mission, values, and roadmapping.
- Come with context. Send an agenda ahead of time, it makes the meeting much more productive.
- I'm not a fan of status-update meetings. If it can be a message, it should be a message. However, if we do not have good async communication on a team, we will supplement with scheduled syncs until remedied.
- When we do meet, I'll be present and engaged - and I expect for the same in return.
- Syncs can still happen over slack with written communication for some small teams that are very clear and consistent with their communication.
